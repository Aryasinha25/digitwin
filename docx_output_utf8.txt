Smart India Hackathon 2026
ThermoRod Twin
A coupled Well-to-Surface Digital Twin for CSS and Sucker Rod Pump optimization in the heavy-oil wells of Baghewala Field




1. Executive summary
Baghewala produces heavy, viscous crude from the Jodhpur Sandstone. Cyclic Steam Stimulation (CSS) heats the near-wellbore zone, and a sucker rod pump (SRP) lifts the oil. Today CSS design and SRP operation are tuned separately, from experience. But they are physically one system: after each steam cycle the zone cools, viscosity rises, and the rods face growing drag. The consequences are rod floating, impact loading, pump unsetting and rod failures.

What we deliver:
A reduced-order thermal model of heating and cooling with a viscosity model calibrated to published field values.
A viscosity-aware rod-string wave-equation solver that predicts rod-float probability, minimum load, impact loading and fatigue damage before any setpoint change.
A state estimator (ensemble Kalman filter) that keeps the twin calibrated from live pump data.
A two-tier optimizer: a slow loop for CSS cycle parameters and a fast constrained model-predictive loop for SPM, stroke and VFD.
Card and motor-power diagnostics, a rod-health score, an economics layer, and a phased deployment path (monitor, advise, supervised control).
A working demo with what-if sliders, plus an ablation study that proves the value of coupling.

2.2 The physical coupling
The chain that the twin models end to end:
Steam injection raises temperature in a heated zone around the well.
During soak and production, the zone cools toward reservoir temperature (46 to 48 deg C).
Viscosity rises steeply as temperature falls (an exponential-type dependence).
Higher viscosity increases drag on the rod string, slowing the rod fall on the downstroke and raising loads on the upstroke.
Result: rod floating, impact loading, fluid pound or poor pump fill, fatigue damage, pump unsetting, and higher energy per barrel.
Falling rates then trigger the next steam cycle, and the decision is made without knowing the rod-side cost.
2.3 Gap today
CSS parameters (steam volume, pressure, soak, cut-off) rest largely on historical practice.
SRP parameters (stroke, SPM, VFD) are adjusted manually and reactively.
Reservoir, wellbore and pump behaviour are never optimized together, so SOR and energy per barrel stay high.

6. Module specifications
6.1 M1: Thermal reduced-order model
Purpose: predict heating during injection and cooling afterward, and convert temperature to viscosity.
Heated zone: growth from injected heat using a Marx-Langenheim / Boberg-Lowe style energy balance. Inputs: steam volume, quality, injection rate, pressure, net pay, formation heat capacity.
Wellbore heat loss: Ramey-type model, with an insulation factor for VIT and thermal wellhead.
Cooling: effective near-wellbore temperature decays toward reservoir temperature.
T(t) = T_res + (T_0 - T_res) * exp(-t / tau)      [tau fitted per well and cycle]
Viscosity: Walther-type relation calibrated to the crude (anchor: 10,000 to 13,000 cP at 50 deg C).
log10(log10(mu + 0.7)) = A - B * log10(T_kelvin)
Inflow: temperature-adjusted productivity index times drawdown, giving rate versus time, then cumulative oil and water.
Limitation stated openly: this is a reduced-order model, not a 3D simulator. Where a CMG-STARS model exists at OIL, M1 parameters can be tuned against it.
6.2 M2: Viscosity-aware rod-string solver
Purpose: predict what the rod string will do for a candidate setpoint, before it is applied.
Model: viscous-damped wave equation along a tapered rod string, with boundary conditions from the polished-rod motion (top) and pump load (bottom).
d2u/dt2 = v^2 * d2u/dx2 - c * du/dt + g

c(x,t) = c_mech + k_geom(x) * mu_eff(T(t))      [proposed form; k_geom from rod/tubing annulus geometry]
Outputs per stroke: simulated surface and pump cards, peak and minimum polished-rod load, rod-section stresses, downhole stroke and fillage, power.
Rod-float indicator: float occurs when viscous drag on the downstroke keeps the rods from following the polished rod down. Indicators are minimum load approaching or falling below the rod buoyant weight margin, downstroke lag between polished rod and pump, and distorted lower loop.
Impact loading: load rate (kN/s) at valve events and at the start of the downstroke, from the solver.
Numerics: finite differences in the style of Everitt-Jennings, sped up with a surrogate for the optimizer loop.
Hydraulic units: a separate actuator model (stroke and speed profile) feeding the same solver.
6.3 M3: State estimator and pump-as-thermometer
Purpose: keep M1 anchored to reality using live pump data.
Per stroke, fit the damping factor that best reconciles the measured surface card with the wave-equation solution (bisection-style iteration, as used in published per-stroke damping methods).
Remove the estimated mechanical part to isolate the viscous part of damping.
Map viscous damping to effective viscosity through the calibrated c to mu relation.
Invert the viscosity model to obtain an effective near-wellbore temperature.
Assimilate into M1 with an ensemble Kalman filter. State: tau, heated volume, T_0 and productivity index. Measurements: effective viscosity from damping, production test rates, wellhead temperature.
Outputs: updated thermal state with uncertainty, and a residual signal that flags model drift or abnormal well behaviour.

6.4 M4: Diagnostics
Inputs: surface and simulated cards where load cells exist, otherwise motor power and current curves.
Where load cells are absent, reconstruct polished-rod torque or load from motor power with a physics-driven model, then classify.
Classes: normal, rod float, fluid pound, gas interference, tagging, worn or leaking valves, pump unsetting precursors, high friction.
Training: cards generated by M2 across conditions, augmented with generative models (GAN or diffusion) following published parallel-learning ideas, then fine-tuned on real cards when OIL data is available.
Also: anomaly detection on power and card features for early unsetting warnings; explainability by highlighting which card region drove the decision.
6.5 M7: Rod reliability
Stress ranges per rod section from M2, converted to allowable stress with a modified Goodman diagram.
S_allow = (0.25 * T_min_tensile + 0.5625 * S_min) * SF        [API-style modified Goodman]
Cumulative fatigue damage with Miner's rule, tracked per well as a live state.
D = sum( n_i / N_i )      failure risk rises as D approaches 1
Outputs: rod-health score, remaining-life estimate, and failure-risk probability from a survival model trained on the failure and unsetting history. Remaining life is a constraint in M5 and M6.
6.6 M5: Slow loop, CSS cycle optimizer
Decision variables: steam volume, injection rate and pressure, soak time, production cut-off. Optional: diluent volume, downhole heater power, if used on the well.
Evaluation: a fast proxy of M1 and M2 (trained on runs of the models), so thousands of candidate cycles evaluate in seconds.
Method: NSGA-II or Bayesian optimization for a Pareto front over four objectives: cumulative oil (maximize), SOR (minimize), energy per barrel (minimize), rod damage (minimize).
Output: a Pareto front the engineer picks from, plus a single recommended cycle with an explanation.
Run when a cycle ends or production nears the cut-off, and for candidate wells for the next cycle.
6.7 M6: Fast loop, SRP setpoint controller
Decision variables: SPM, stroke length, VFD frequency (or stroke and speed profile for hydraulic units).
Method: constrained model predictive control using M2 as the model over a short horizon (hours to days), with the M1 cooling forecast supplying the expected viscosity trajectory.
Objective: maximize produced fluid and pump fillage while minimizing energy and fatigue damage.
Hard constraints: rod stress below Goodman allowable in every section, minimum load above float threshold, load-rate limit, SPM and VFD limits, pump fillage above a minimum, structural unit limits.
Behaviour: as viscosity is predicted to rise, SPM is slowed in advance to keep rods falling freely, and speeds up while the zone is hot. This replaces reactive manual adjustment.
Mode: advisory first; supervised automatic control only after operators trust the recommendations.
6.8 M8: Economics and impact model
J = oil * price - steam * steam_cost - energy * tariff - P(failure) * workover_cost
All recommendations show rupees per barrel, kWh per barrel, SOR, avoided failures and avoided workover cost.
Cost parameters are inputs that OIL can set. We use clearly labeled placeholders.

8. Data strategy
8.1 Data we expect from OIL
Production history, CSS cycle records, steam injection parameters, VFD and SRP operating data, rod failure and pump unsetting history, completion and reservoir data, fluid properties and pressure data (as listed in the PS).
8.2 If real data is not available in time
Build a synthetic multi-well, multi-cycle generator from M1 and M2 with sensor noise, missing data, drift and injected events (float, fluid pound, gas interference, unsetting, rod failure) at realistic frequencies.
Augment cards with generative models to widen coverage.
State clearly in every result that it comes from synthetic data. Use the same pipeline to retrain on OIL data later.
Anchor the synthetic world to public facts: viscosity range, depth, reservoir temperature, published production levels.
8.3 Data quality
Automated checks for stuck or flat sensors, out-of-range values, timestamp gaps, unit mismatches, and card sanity (closed loop, plausible loads). Bad data is flagged and excluded rather than silently used.
9. Validation and evaluation plan
9.1 Model validation

9.2 Ablation: proving that coupling matters
Same synthetic wells and same failure physics, three operating policies:
Baseline: fixed CSS design and manual, reactive SRP settings (representing current practice).
SRP-only optimization: fast loop active, CSS design unchanged.
Coupled: both loops active with the thermal forecast shared.
Metrics: cumulative oil, SOR, kWh per barrel, float events, unsetting events per year, rod damage, cost per barrel. The gap between policies 2 and 3 is the evidence for the coupling thesis. Report as ranges over many random seeds, not a single run.
9.3 Uncertainty and sensitivity
Ensembles for M1 and M3 give confidence bands on temperature, viscosity and rate.
Sensitivity plots for the most influential parameters (tau, viscosity slope, damping map).
Robustness test: rerun the ablation with wrong model parameters to show that the estimator recovers and the optimizer stays safe.
